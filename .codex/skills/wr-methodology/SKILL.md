# Design and Evaluation Methodology Writing

Load `wr-general` once. Sources: Wobbrock's Design and Method sections; CHI guidance on collection and analysis. Use only the blocks applicable to the realised study.

## Architecture

Open with a compact RQ → method → data → outcome map. Present formative work, prototype/stimuli, study design, participants, apparatus, procedure, measures, preparation, and analysis in the order needed to understand and reproduce the work. State each stage's purpose before its mechanics; report what was done without importing findings.

## Designed artefact

- Explain the goals, principles, important properties, appearance, behaviour, and intended use conditions.
- Connect consequential design choices to rationale, alternatives or trade-offs, and relevant formative process; omit discarded history unless it explains the realised design.
- Describe how it works and is built well enough for an expert to reproduce it. Emphasise the ideas and their realisation; include platform, code, or engineering details only when they affect behaviour or replication.
- Use diagrams and figures to show structure and interaction, preferably near their first reference when layout permits. Avoid unnecessary heading depth.

## Quantitative study

- **Participants:** recruited and analysed counts, sampling/access, relevant characteristics, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and compensation; report only documented details.
- **Apparatus:** equipment/software, arrangement, stimuli and condition mappings, and versions or performance parameters that matter for replication.
- **Procedure:** arrival-to-departure tasks, order/counterbalancing, instructions, timing, repetitions, and the definition of a trial.
- **Design and analysis:** factors and levels, within-/between-participant structure, outcomes and units, collected/retained observations, exclusions and reasons, preprocessing and aggregation, and approved analysis operations. Name statistical tests, corrections, or assumptions only if actually used; descriptive analysis does not imply inferential testing.

## Qualitative study

- Describe participants' roles and setting, access and sampling, and any theory that actually shaped these choices.
- Explain collection: questions, probes, observations or artefacts, recording, researcher role, and duration as applicable.
- Explain analysis: unit of coding, code development and application, illustrative codes or codebook reference, who coded, and how interpretations were checked.
- Report agreement, reflexivity, validation, or grounded-theory procedures only when performed; never adopt a method label because it appears in a writing example.

For mixed methods, explain each strand and where they connect. Keep rationale beside the decision, use tables for repeated mappings, cross-reference shared setup, and preserve every operational detail needed for an expert to repeat collection and analysis.
