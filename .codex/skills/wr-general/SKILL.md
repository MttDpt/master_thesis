# General Scientific Writing

Apply once before the matching `wr-*` skill; load only the skill for the passage being written. The user's request and thesis requirements control scope and format.

## Reader and argument

- Write concise British English for a reader who does not know the study. Decide what the reader should understand, remember, and be able to do with the findings.
- Organise around one central contribution: real problem, approach, evidence, bounded contribution. A missing paper is not sufficient motivation; explain why the unanswered question matters to people or the field.
- Use context–content–conclusion at paper, section, and paragraph scales: orient the reader, supply evidence or reasoning, resolve the question. Give each paragraph one job; omit a closing sentence if it merely repeats the opening.
- Present the logic supporting the claim, not the history of doing the research. Keep related ideas together, cover each supporting topic in one place, and use parallel syntax for parallel claims.
- Define necessary technical terms at first use; reuse the same term for the same concept. Minimise acronyms, unexplained assumptions, and threads readers must remember.

## Golden rule: meaning ratio

**Meaning ratio = meaningful words / total words.** Increase it by removing words that add no meaning, not by removing evidence or making prose cryptic; this is an editing heuristic, not a numerical score.

- Use familiar words and concrete verbs: “utilises” → “uses”; “in order to” → “to”. Replace jargon and unnecessary technical language; explain indispensable terminology plainly.
- Cut throat-clearing, rhetorical emphasis, duplicated definitions, and announcements of what the next sentence says.
- Preserve citations, exact denominators, exclusions, uncertainty, null findings, limitations, and details needed for replication. Never strengthen a claim to make it punchier.
- Distinguish observation, supported interpretation, and speculation. “First”, “only”, “clearly shows”, and causal language require evidence; they are not stock phrases.
- Describe procedures impersonally without “the researcher” or “the experimenter”; combine sentences only while preserving tasks, order, counts, and timing.
- Let visuals carry detailed values; prose explains the relevant pattern. Embed cross-references in the sentence that uses the evidence.

## Study evidence and targeted edits

- Put participants and their actions first. Explain measures with concrete wording, such as time looking at the display or distance from the lane centre; use each measure's verified event window and label.
- Name every reported quantity, unit, and summary statistic. Use `Mdn=` for medians and `(n=)` when the relevant count is otherwise missing; distinguish trials, evaluable windows, detected events, and presses without repeating an already clear denominator.
- Apply requested annotation replacements only to the selected text; preserve surrounding prose and explicit deletions. Answer a question about a measure's identity directly. Flag substantive factual conflicts briefly rather than silently changing scientific definitions or broadly rewriting the passage.
- Before drafting, revising, or discussing empirical study findings in any chapter, including Results and Discussion, read [study-results wording](references/study-results-wording.md). It holds the shared terminology, comparison patterns, and annotation examples; analysis contracts and approved reports remain authoritative for definitions, timings, and values.

## Efficient application

- Start from the requested passage, approved reports, and relevant literature; retrieve more only for a concrete gap. Do not rerun analyses or regenerate reports to draft prose.
- For substantial drafting, sketch each paragraph's purpose before polishing sentences; for a small edit, work directly. Check problem → evidence → contribution before sentence economy.
- Use an informative, memorable title without cuteness or inflated novelty. Follow the thesis template for headings, keywords, references, and acknowledgements; verify actual assistance and funding.
- Use `wr-abstract`, `wr-introduction`, `wr-background`, `wr-methodology`, `wr-results`, `wr-discussion`, or `wr-conclusion` only for the relevant passage.
- A chat draft needs no publication or build. Apply requested TeX changes through `overleaf-local-edit` and verify affected output through `overleaf-build-audit`.
- Stop when the passage is accurate, coherent, concise, and evidence-bounded. Feedback changes the passage; reusable rules change only when requested.

Source basis: Mensh–Kording rules 2–4, Nacke/Korte's CHI notes, and Wobbrock. Read [source mapping and adaptations](references/source-map.md) only to trace a rule or resolve differing advice.
