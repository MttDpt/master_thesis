# APA numbers and statistics

Apply the rules in the American Psychological Association (2024), *APA Style numbers and statistics guide* (7th edition; updated September 11, 2024), pp. 1–2, when writing or editing numbers and statistical reporting. This skill contains only that source's rules.

The guide points to Publication Manual Sections 6.32–6.35 for numerals and words, Section 6.36 for decimals, Sections 6.40–6.45 for reporting statistics, and Table 6.5 for statistical symbols and abbreviations.

## Numerals and words

- Use numerals for numbers 10 and above, except the word-form cases below.
- Use numerals regardless of size for statistics (e.g., 2.45, 3 times as many, a 2 × 2 design), measurements with units (e.g., a 7-mg dose), times, ages, dates, scale scores and points (e.g., a score of 6, a 5-point Likert scale), exact sums of money, and numbers referred to as numerals (e.g., the numeral 4).
- Use numerals for positions in numbered series (e.g., Grade 6, Items 2 and 3, Row 4), parts of books (e.g., Chapter 1), and table and figure numbers.
- Use words for zero through nine unless a numeral rule above applies.
- Spell out numbers beginning a sentence, heading, or title; also use words for common fractions (e.g., one half, a two-thirds majority) and universally accepted phrases (e.g., Twelve Apostles, Five Pillars of Islam).
- Combine words and numerals to distinguish adjacent numerical modifiers (e.g., ten 7-point scales, 2 two-way interactions).

## Commas and plurals

- Separate groups of three digits with commas in most numbers of 1,000 or more.
- Omit these commas in page numbers, binary digits, serial numbers, degrees of temperature, degrees of freedom, and acoustic frequencies above 1000.
- Form plural numerals and number words with s or es, without an apostrophe (e.g., fours, sixes, 1950s); the guide also permits plural statistical letter names such as Ms and ps.
- Do not add plural endings to symbols or measurement abbreviations (e.g., 3 cm, not 3 cms); plural statistical letter names follow the guide's separate Ms/ps convention above.

## Decimals and precision

- Use a leading zero for values below 1 when the statistic can exceed 1 (e.g., M = 0.5).
- Omit the leading zero when the statistic cannot exceed 1, including proportions, correlations, and significance levels (e.g., r = .42, p = .015).
- As a general default, report means and standard deviations from integer-scale data, such as questionnaires, to one decimal place.
- As a general default, report other means and standard deviations, correlations, proportions, and inferential statistics such as t, F, and chi-square to two decimal places.
- Report exact p-values to two or three decimal places, except values below .001, which use p < .001.
- Round as much as the intended use and statistical precision permit; retain additional decimals when the defaults would mislead, obscure a meaningful difference, or misrepresent a significance boundary.

## LaTeX count formatting

- Format sample sizes and counts in inline math mode, e.g. `$n=72$`; when giving a numerator and denominator, use `$n=3/7$` (three of seven). Apply this in thesis prose and captions, keeping surrounding words and punctuation outside the math delimiters.

## Reporting statistics

- Do not repeat the same statistics in both prose and a table or figure.
- In tables and figures, report exact p-values (e.g., p = .015), except values below .001, which are reported as p < .001.
- Put spaces on both sides of mathematical operators (e.g., M = 7.7, p < .001, 2 × 2). Attach a unary minus directly to its negative value (e.g., −8.25), with no space after the minus.
- Use statistical symbols or abbreviations with mathematical operators (e.g., M = 7.7); use the term in narrative prose (e.g., “the means were”).
- Italicize Latin letters used as statistical symbols or algebraic variables, including when they occur in narrative text (e.g., t-test, p-values, R²). Keep Greek letters upright, not italic.
- Do not define conventional statistical symbols or abbreviations such as M, SD, F, t, df, p, N, n, and OR, or those composed of Greek letters.
- Define other abbreviations at first use, including AIC, ANOVA, BIC, CFA, CI, NFI, RMSEA, and SEM.
