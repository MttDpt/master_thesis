# Conclusion and Future Work Writing

Load `wr-general` once. Sources: Wobbrock's Conclusion and Future Work; CHI contribution focus; Mensh–Kording rule 8's implications.

- Close the problem-to-contribution story opened in the Introduction: answer the aim and RQs compactly, affirm which promises were fulfilled, and state the delivered contribution.
- Zoom out from individual findings to the knowledge, method, artefact, or design understanding the work provides. Avoid re-reporting numerical results unless one is indispensable to the takeaway.
- Make the value intelligible beyond the narrow topic while keeping it within the realised population, setting, measures, and analytical boundaries.
- Include only the most consequential limitation needed to qualify that value; do not repeat the Discussion.
- Add no new evidence, methods, literature arguments, interpretations, or unsupported novelty claims.
- For future work, prioritise one or two substantive intellectual directions arising from demonstrated limitations or unresolved findings. Explain what each would establish and why it matters; avoid an incremental implementation TODO list.
- End on the contribution and its bounded significance: what is better understood or enabled because the work was done. “More research is needed” is not the main answer to “so what?”

Preserve the thesis template's chapter title and structure; Wobbrock's preference for singular “Conclusion” does not require renaming an established thesis chapter.
