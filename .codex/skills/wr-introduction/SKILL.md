# Introduction Writing

Load `wr-general` once. Sources: Mensh–Kording rule 6; CHI territory–niche–response guidance; Wobbrock's five-part introduction.

## Argument

1. **Establish the territory:** describe the relevant world, define the problem and essential concepts, and explain concrete stakes for people or practice.
2. **Establish the niche:** move from relevant knowns to a precise unknown, limitation, or opportunity; show why resolving it matters independently of its absence from the literature.
3. **Occupy the niche:** state the thesis aim and canonical RQs, then briefly explain the approach taken to answer them.
4. **Preview key findings:** give the approved answers at a higher level than Results, so the reader can see what the approach delivered.
5. **Name the contributions:** state the knowledge or artefacts actually delivered and their scope; usually one to three substantive contributions suffice.

## Execution

- Treat every Introduction chapter, section, and subsection as a map of the research landscape relevant to this study, not as a collection of everything broadly related to the topic.
- End each chapter, section, and subsection by stating where this study fits, which gap it addresses, and how it contributes to or changes that landscape.
- Keep a theory, technology, vehicle example, or prior study only when it passes at least one relevance test:
  - it is directly relevant to an RQ;
  - it informed the study or prototype design; or
  - the results will engage with, support, or challenge it.
- Remove material that passes none of these tests, especially broad cognitive-theory explanations and surveys of vehicle implementations that do not directly support the work.
- Use progressively narrower paragraphs: topic/context → relevant prior knowledge → the consequential unknown. Keep only literature needed to motivate the work; thematic review belongs in Background.
- Explain relevant challenges and constraints for the people designing or using the system, rather than relying on broad claims of importance.
- Summarise the method at design level, not as a procedure. Preserve canonical RQ wording and state it once.
- Make findings and contributions answer the gap established above. Every promise must be delivered later; do not promise general effectiveness from bounded descriptive evidence.
- Close on the contribution's value, not merely future research. Add a brief roadmap only when required or helpful, without repeating the argument.

Useful transitions, when supported: “Prior work has established …”; “It remains unclear whether …”; “To address this gap, this study …”. Use actual findings, not placeholder claims or invented novelty.
