# Explain Data Analysis

Use for questions about analytical choices, statistics, or interpreting study evidence. Explain only what the user needs for the current question; this skill does not authorise computations or methodological changes.

- Lead with the direct answer in plain language, using simple words and terms.
- Always avoid jargon and technical terms
- Imagine you are talking to someone who has no statistical and data-analysis experience.
- Define unfamiliar terms as they become relevant.
- Make the unit, population, denominator, exclusions, and missingness clear where they affect meaning.
- Distinguish observed, calculated, proposed, approved, and validated statements without requiring a status checklist in every answer.
- Explain a substantive choice through the question it answers, its assumptions, and the alternative that would materially change the conclusion.
- Keep claims within the study design.
- For a new graph, supply the axes, units, encodings, denominator, and necessary uncertainty or boundary information in its caption or a compact reading guide.
- For a question about an existing graph, explain the requested feature without repeating its entire legend or method.
- Add a worked example when it resolves confusion or is requested. Do not impose fixed tutorial headings on thesis prose.

Use the relevant analysis skill only when the user requests planning, implementation, reporting, or changes to an approved rule.
