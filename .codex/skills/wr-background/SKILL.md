# Background and Related-Work Writing

Load `wr-general` once. Source: Wobbrock's Related Work; apply the shared logical-flow rules.

- Answer four questions for each relevant body of work: who investigated it, what they did, what they found, and how this thesis differs.
- Organise by concepts, questions, or themes the thesis needs, not one paper or author at a time. Teach the reader what the literature collectively establishes.
- Within each theme, move through governing concept → synthesised evidence → unresolved boundary → consequence for this thesis's design, method, RQ, or interpretation.
- Compare only along useful dimensions, such as task, signal, placement, method, measure, or limitation. State agreement and disagreement with citations attached to the claims.
- Differentiate at theme level through the question, approach, technology, setting, or findings. Difference need not mean superiority; describe replication honestly when it is the purpose.
- Include enough information about methods and findings to make the comparison meaningful. An application catalogue or citation list without synthesis does not establish a gap.
- Retain foundational concepts needed later, but remove material that merely demonstrates reading coverage. Cross-reference established definitions.
- End with one synthesis of what is known, what remains unresolved, and why the thesis follows; avoid repeated mini-conclusions.

Check: can a reader explain both the relevant prior knowledge and this thesis's distinctive, bounded contribution without reconstructing the argument from citations?
