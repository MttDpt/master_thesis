# Discussion Writing

Load `wr-general` once and interpret approved results without reopening settled analyses. Sources: Mensh–Kording rule 8; CHI introduction–evaluation–conclusion structure; Wobbrock's Discussion.

- Open with a compact synthesis of the major findings and their meaning: how far was the Introduction's problem or gap addressed?
- Organise by RQ or contribution, not by figure. For each substantive point, connect the answer → exact supporting finding → comparison with literature → plausible explanation → boundary → proportionate implication.
- Evaluate strengths as well as weaknesses. Explain how the evidence supports the quality or usefulness of the proposed approach, what worked, and what did not.
- Compare with relevant literature at the level of findings, mechanisms, or conditions. Address hypotheses or theoretical predictions only when they were actually posed.
- Examine unexpected, null, and inconclusive findings and credible alternative explanations. Distinguish measured evidence from inferred mechanisms; “one explanation is …” must remain a qualified interpretation.
- Make limitations constrain claims: identify the issue, which conclusion it restricts, and the resulting boundary. Consolidate measurement, internal-validity, population, setting, and temporal limits instead of adding a generic disclaimer list.
- Derive implications and recommendations at the strength supported by the study design; a descriptive pattern alone does not establish causal benefit or broad effectiveness.
- Close by explaining what the contribution now makes possible and which important question remains, rather than repeating every result or making more research the sole value.

Use as much structure as the thesis argument needs; the papers' short discussion examples and page ratios are not thesis length requirements.
