## Thesis research aim

This thesis investigates whether peripheral ambient displays can support driver awareness through subtle, non-semantic, metaphor-based visual communication while minimising distraction and preserving attention on the road.

The research questions evaluate a progressive chain of peripheral communication: detection, discrimination, and interpretation.

## Research questions

### RQ1: Detection

How effectively do drivers detect changes in a peripheral ambient display, and how much visual attention does the display receive?

RQ1 asks how effectively drivers detect a visual change while driving and how much visual attention the display receives. It is the first condition for peripheral communication.

### RQ2: Discrimination

How accurately and confidently can drivers discriminate whether and what changed in the peripheral ambient display?

RQ2 asks whether drivers can distinguish no-change from change and identify a change by its type, location, or visual category, while reporting their confidence. It is the second condition for peripheral communication.

### RQ3: Interpretation

How do drivers interpret the intended meaning of metaphor-based visual changes in a peripheral ambient display?

RQ3 asks how drivers make meaning from a peripheral visual transformation, including ambiguity and misinterpretation. It is the third condition for peripheral communication.

## Relationship between the research questions

| Layer | Question | Core concern |
|---|---|---|
| Detection | RQ1 | Detection effectiveness and display visual attention |
| Discrimination | RQ2 | Recognition of whether and what changed, with reported confidence |
| Interpretation | RQ3 | Meaning, misinterpretation, and confidence |

Together, the questions assess whether a peripheral display can function as a communication system rather than only as a visual effect.

## Contribution framing

The RQs frame contributions to automotive HCI, peripheral and ambient displays, and metaphor-based interface design by examining how peripheral visual signals are noticed, differentiated, and interpreted.
